Mission Overview

What We Are Planning

The Brazil Mission Trip 2027 will bring a team of approximately 10 dental students, 5 faculty members, and a dental hygienist from Augusta University to Quixadá, Ceará, Brazil for a multi-day humanitarian dental outreach mission. Partnering once again with Centro Universitário Católica de Quixadá (Unicatólica), the team will provide comprehensive dental care — restorative treatment, periodontics, oral surgery, diagnostic imaging, and oral health education — to underserved communities in the semi-arid Sertão region, where access to dental care is critically limited.

This mission builds on the extraordinary success of the 2022 Brazil Mission Trip, which was organized between the University of Detroit Mercy and Unicatólica. That mission treated 474 patients and performed 2,396 procedures in just four days — establishing a proven framework, community relationships, and logistical infrastructure that the 2027 mission will leverage and expand. Full data from the 2022 mission is documented later in this report as evidence of feasibility and impact.

Proposed Team Composition

10 dental students from Augusta University's Dental College of Georgia, 5 faculty members (licensed supervising dentists), and 1 dental hygienist providing preventive care and oral health education. The team will partner with Brazilian students and faculty from Unicatólica, effectively doubling clinical capacity on the ground. All patient care will be supervised by licensed faculty from both institutions.

Confirmed Window — Summer Break 2027

The mission will take place during Augusta University’s three-week summer break: June 14 – July 5, 2027. We are currently coordinating with Unicatólica and local partners to determine which week within this window works best for all parties. The three options under consideration:

Week A — June 14–20  |  Week B — June 21–27  |  Week C — June 28–July 5

The trip itself is expected to span approximately 10 days — including travel to and from Fortaleza, ground transit to Quixadá, 4–6 clinical service days, orientation, and a cultural exchange day. The final schedule will depend on faculty availability, Brazilian academic calendar alignment, flight logistics, and community readiness in Quixadá.

The Setting

Quixadá, Ceará — The Heart of the Sertão

Quixadá (from the indigenous Tupi language, meaning “rock corral” or “curral de pedras”) is a municipality of approximately 87,000 people located in the Sertão Central — the semi-arid interior of Ceará state, roughly 170 km (105 miles) south of the capital Fortaleza. Known locally as the “Land of Monoliths” for its dramatic inselberg rock formations, including the iconic Pedra da Galinha Choca (Brooding Hen Rock), Quixadá sits in one of the most arid regions of Brazil, where the Caatinga biome dominates — a landscape of thorny shrubland, cacti, and sparse vegetation adapted to prolonged drought.

The Sertão has historically been one of Brazil’s most economically disadvantaged regions. Irregular rainfall, poor soil quality, and limited infrastructure have perpetuated cycles of poverty — particularly in the rural districts surrounding Quixadá. Communities like Residencial Raquel de Queiroz and São João dos Queiroz, where the 2022 mission team provided care, are located far from urban health centers. Many residents have never seen a dentist, resulting in severe untreated dental disease, infections, and tooth loss that deeply impacts nutrition, social participation, and quality of life.

Why These Communities Are Underserved

Brazil’s Sertão region faces a convergence of challenges that make healthcare delivery extremely difficult. The semi-arid climate produces chronic droughts that can last years, concentrating poverty in rural settlements. While Brazil’s public health system (SUS) provides basic medical coverage, dental care remains critically scarce in rural Ceará. The nearest specialist may be hours away on unpaved roads. Many families survive on subsistence farming and government aid programs. The average annual rainfall is only 400–700mm, and temperatures regularly exceed 35°C (95°F). The Caatinga biome — the only biome exclusive to Brazil — defines the landscape: drought-resistant vegetation, rocky outcrops, and seasonal rivers that run dry for months.

About Ceará State

Ceará is a state of striking contrasts. Its 573 km Atlantic coastline features world-famous beaches and tourism destinations, while its interior remains one of the poorest regions in the Americas. The state’s HDI varies dramatically — coastal cities like Fortaleza (pop. 2.7M) enjoy modern infrastructure, while interior municipalities lag far behind in healthcare, education, and income indicators.

The Contrast: Coast vs. Interior

Fortaleza, the state capital and 5th largest city in Brazil, is a major tourism hub and the gateway airport (Pinto Martins International) for the mission team. Nearby attractions like Beach Park (Latin America’s largest water park complex) and the world-renowned Jericoacoara beach destination draw global visitors — yet just 170 km inland, communities in the Sertão lack basic dental care. This mission bridges that gap.

Budget & Logistics

Affordable Impact — The Exchange Rate Advantage

One of the most compelling aspects of this mission is its extraordinary cost-efficiency. The current USD-to-BRL exchange rate of approximately 1 USD = 5.0 BRL means that local expenses in Brazil — accommodation, meals, and incidentals — are remarkably affordable for American participants. A quality hotel room in Quixadá costs roughly R$120–180 per night (approximately $24–36 USD), and a full meal at a local restaurant runs R$20–40 (approximately $4–8 USD).

The primary cost for each student is international airfare, estimated at approximately $1,000 USD round-trip (Augusta → Fortaleza, typically routed through São Paulo or Miami). All ground transportation in Brazil is provided at no cost to participants — a dedicated charter bus will transport the team between Fortaleza, Quixadá, and each service location. Local authorities, including police escorts and ambulance support, will accompany the team to and from rural outreach sites, ensuring safety and logistical coordination throughout the mission. With corporate sponsorship offsetting materials, equipment, and supply costs, the per-student out-of-pocket expense for a week-long international clinical mission is remarkably accessible.

What This Means

At the current rate, every US dollar buys approximately 5 Brazilian Reais. This makes Brazil one of the most cost-effective destinations for international dental missions. A full day of meals costs less than a single lunch in Augusta. Hotel rooms that would cost $150+ in the US run under $35 in Quixadá. This favorable exchange rate dramatically lowers the barrier to participation for students.

On-the-Ground Logistics — Provided at No Cost

Local partners in Quixadá will coordinate all in-country transportation and safety logistics. A dedicated charter bus will shuttle the entire team between Fortaleza airport, the hotel in Quixadá, and each clinical site — including the remote rural schools that are inaccessible by regular transit. The Quixadá Municipal Government and local police will provide escort vehicles for travel to rural districts, ensuring safe passage on unpaved roads. Ambulance support will be on standby at each outreach location for medical emergencies. The municipal Secretaria de Saúde (Health Department) will handle community pre-screening, patient scheduling, and site preparation. None of these services carry any cost to participating students or faculty.

2022 Mission Results

Executive Summary — 2022 Mission Data

In August 2022, dental students and faculty from the University of Detroit Mercy traveled to Quixadá, partnering with Unicatólica. Over four consecutive days, the combined teams delivered comprehensive dental care to 474 patients, performing 2,396 clinical procedures across five treatment categories. The mission was supported by 3M (materials sponsorship), a NOMAD Pro 2 portable X-ray unit (manufacturer loaner), and the Alpha Omega International Dental Society (founded 1907 — the oldest international dental organization). The data was tabulated using SPSS Statistics 28.0.1 and subsequently published in Revista Expressão Católica (v. 11, special edition, November 2022, ISSN: 2357-8483).

Key Clinical Insight From 2022

Day 1 (Residencial Raquel de Queiroz) had an unusually high referral rate of 21.9% — nearly six times the mission average of 4.0%. This most remote location presented patients with significantly more advanced dental disease that exceeded field treatment capacity. By Day 4 (Escola São João dos Queiroz), the referral rate dropped to just 1.8%, suggesting greater field efficiency and more effective pre-screening. The dramatic X-ray increase from Day 1 (3.3%) to Days 2–4 (17%+) reflects the transition from rudimentary rural settings to the better-equipped campus where the NOMAD portable units could operate more effectively. These learnings will directly inform the 2027 mission’s planning and resource allocation.

Looking Forward

Join the 2027 Mission

The 2022 Brazil Mission Trip proved that a small, well-organized team can deliver transformative dental care to hundreds of patients in just days — working on school desks with flashlights, using portable equipment carried across continents. That mission treated 474 patients, performed 2,396 procedures, and was published in a peer-reviewed journal. Now we have the opportunity to build on that foundation.

The 2027 Brazil Mission Trip from Augusta University represents the next chapter of this partnership — bringing more students, more resources, and refined logistics to the communities of Quixadá who need it most. With strong institutional backing, a favorable exchange rate making the mission remarkably affordable, and a proven operational model, we are positioned to deliver even greater impact.

Whether you are a student looking to gain invaluable clinical and cultural experience, a faculty member committed to service-learning, or a sponsor seeking a high-impact, well-documented partnership — there is a place for you in this mission.
